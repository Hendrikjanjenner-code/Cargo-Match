# CARGOMATCH — DEEPSEA + MULTIMODAL MASTER EXTENSION V3

Extend the EXISTING CargoMatch application.

IMPORTANT:

Do NOT rebuild the application from scratch.

Do NOT remove existing functionality.

Do NOT replace the existing privacy, authentication, company, matching, messaging, network or AIS architecture unless a migration is explicitly required.

Build on top of the existing CargoMatch application.

The objective of this extension is to evolve CargoMatch from an inland-waterway capacity marketplace into a long-term:

# TRANSPORT CAPACITY INTELLIGENCE NETWORK

CargoMatch should eventually support:

1. INLAND WATERWAY
2. DEEPSEA DRY BULK
3. CONVENTIONAL / BREAKBULK
4. LINER SHIPPING
5. ROAD FREIGHT
6. RAIL FREIGHT
7. AIR CARGO
8. LCL / GROUPAGE IMPORT WORKFLOWS

However:

DO NOT attempt to fully build every transport mode at once.

Design multimodal.

Build and validate vertical slices.

---

# 1. CORE CARGOMATCH PRINCIPLE

CargoMatch should answer one fundamental commercial question:

**What suitable transport capacity is going to become available, where and when, and what cargo can use it next?**

The platform should increasingly identify opportunities BEFORE transport capacity becomes empty, idle or unnecessarily repositioned.

Universal flow:

CURRENT OPERATION

↓

EXPECTED COMPLETION

↓

EXPECTED AVAILABILITY

↓

COMMERCIAL CAPACITY

↓

ELIGIBLE CARGO

↓

MATCH

↓

INTEREST

↓

MUTUAL ACCEPTANCE

↓

CONNECTED

↓

COMMERCIAL DISCUSSION

CargoMatch facilitates commercial discovery and introductions.

CONNECTED does NOT mean:

BOOKED

FIXED

CONTRACTED

RESERVED

or

TRANSPORT CONFIRMED.

---

# 2. TWO-SIDED MARKETPLACE

CargoMatch must always work in both directions.

CAPACITY → CARGO

Example:

"My vessel will become open in Malaysia."

↓

"What suitable cargo is available nearby?"

And:

CARGO → CAPACITY

Example:

"I have 75,000 mt loading Indonesia."

↓

"What suitable vessels are expected to become open nearby?"

This principle applies across every transport mode.

# 8. OPERATIONAL SIGNAL ≠ COMMERCIAL AVAILABILITY

This rule applies throughout CargoMatch.

AIS shows a vessel at anchor.

That does NOT prove the vessel is commercially open.

GPS shows a truck stationary.

That does NOT prove the truck is empty.

Rail data shows wagons at a terminal.

That does NOT prove they are commercially available.

Flight tracking shows an aircraft flying.

That does NOT prove there is cargo space for sale.

Therefore separate:

OPERATIONAL / TELEMETRY DATA

from:

COMMERCIAL CAPACITY DATA

Commercial availability must come from:

authorized user confirmation

or

trusted commercial system integration.

# PART B — DEEPSEA DRY BULK

# 12. DEEPSEA CORE PROBLEM

Deepsea vessels can finish voyages far away from their next known employment.

This may result in significant ballast repositioning.

Example:

An owner has fixed a vessel on a voyage.

The vessel eventually discharges in Southeast Asia.

Its next known employment would require substantial ballast repositioning.

Meanwhile a charterer may have suitable cargo near the vessel's future open position.

CargoMatch should identify that commercial opportunity BEFORE the vessel finishes its current voyage.

Core flow:

CURRENT VOYAGE

↓

EXPECTED DISCHARGE

↓

FUTURE OPEN POSITION

↓

SUITABLE FUTURE CARGO

↓

ELIGIBILITY

↓

MATCH

↓

COMMERCIAL INTRODUCTION
